# 0023 — Phases 0 and 1 approved; a retail, educational site in blue and white; launch on Whop without asking first; the rebalancing lab

- **Date:** 2026-10-21
- **Status:** Accepted (the owners' answers, 2026-10-21)

## Decision

1. **Phase 0 and Phase 1 are approved**, with the changes below. Phase 1 is `docs/plan.md` §9 as revised here.
2. **The audience is everyday (retail) investors, and the site must read as education.** The dense terminal look
   shown earlier the same day was "too technical"; it showed how much information the product can carry, not how it
   should look. The site is now white and blue, with serif headings, plain sentences before numbers, a short "What this
   means" note beside each figure, and a numbered path of lessons. Deeper pages (an asset's full detail, the method
   pages) keep their depth, written as plainly as their subject allows.
3. **No advance request to Whop.** The owners will not write to Whop support before launching: Whop reviews every
   business at sign-up (website, what customers receive, renewal and refund terms) and again on a schedule, and they
   judge that an extra request would add nothing. The product stays plainly educational (hard rule 3 unchanged).
   What Whop does if it doubts a business (paused payouts, reserves, suspension) is in plan §12.1; keeping the site,
   the Whop listing and all marketing educational, with no earnings claims, is the protection.
4. **The rebalancing lab is chosen** (`docs/minilizard-gap.md`, version B): hypothetical baskets and rules, tested on
   past prices against not rebalancing, with no real holdings and no suggestions. It moves into Phase 2 to be built;
   it is shown publicly only after the lawyer's view (plan §5), like the 0–1 band position.
5. **Hosting.** GitHub Pages cannot host the product: GitHub's terms say Pages is not "a free web-hosting service to
   run your online business, e-commerce site, or any other website that is primarily directed at either facilitating
   commercial transactions or providing commercial software as a service", and Pages serves only fixed files, so it
   could not keep paid pages from free visitors (hard rule 4). The code stays on GitHub and deploys from it; the
   public site goes to a host whose free plan allows commercial use (plan §12.5).

## Consequences

- The owners set up the Whop business themselves (plan §12.1: one of them creates it and invites the other as a second
  Owner; the first linked payout account is treated by Whop as the owner of all revenue, so they choose it on purpose).
- The launch checklist (plan §9) no longer includes Whop's written approval. It still includes a price source whose
  terms allow display (hard rule 2; plan §12.2) and the lawyer's view (hard rule 3).
